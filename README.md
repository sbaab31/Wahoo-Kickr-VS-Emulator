## 🧭 Overview

There is a quiet tragedy in the world of indoor cycling: a perfectly healthy smart trainer, mechanically sound, electronically capable, suddenly rendered "outdated" because a firmware branch stopped shipping. The **KICKR Legacy Motion Bridge (KLMB)** exists to argue with that fate — and win.

KLMB is an independent, community-minded software layer that re-imagines what a legacy Wahoo KICKR smart trainer can do when paired with modern cycling software. Instead of asking you to purchase new hardware, KLMB focuses on translating, bridging, and interpreting the signals your trainer *already sends* into the virtual shifting experience modern riders now expect.

Think of it as a translator standing between two people who speak related but incompatible dialects: your trainer speaks "resistance-ohms-and-watts," your riding app speaks "virtual-gear-and-cadence," and KLMB makes sure the conversation flows without awkward pauses mid-sprint.

---

## 🎯 Why This Project Exists

When virtual shifting arrived on the scene, it felt like magic: tap a button, feel the terrain change, climb without touching a derailleur cable. But that magic was gated behind firmware updates that only reached a subset of trainer models. Riders on older KICKR units were left watching from the sidelines — full of watts, short on options.

KLMB was born from a simple, stubborn question: *why should great hardware be retired by software policy?*

This repository is the answer — an open, extendable bridge that gives legacy trainers a second act.

## 🧠 How It Works (Conceptually)

KLMB doesn't pretend to be firmware. It sits beside your existing setup like a thoughtful co-pilot.

1. **Listen** — It observes incoming virtual shifting requests from your riding application.
2. **Interpret** — It maps those requests onto a resistance curve appropriate for your trainer's capabilities.
3. **Relay** — It sends adjusted resistance targets to the trainer over the connection method you've chosen.
4. **Confirm** — It reads back the trainer's response to ensure the gear change actually landed.
5. **Adapt** — It learns your preferences over time, smoothing shifts that felt too aggressive or too sluggish.

The result is a ride that feels intentional, not accidental.
